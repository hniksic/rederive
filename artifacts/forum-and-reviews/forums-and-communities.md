# Forum threads and retrocomputing communities

## WinWorld forum - "Derive 3.x" discussion

Source: https://forum.winworldpc.com/discussion/14355/derive-3-x

- **System** (the forum's auto-generated intro, November 2022) introduces
  Derive as "a computer algebra and graphing system, developed as a
  successor to muMATH by Soft Warehouse, Inc." and notes it was
  discontinued June 29, 2007, with Derive 6.1 for Windows being the final
  release.
- **whisperingmodem** (Nov 2022) suggests other CAS-adjacent systems worth
  exploring for comparison/preservation purposes: the HP 48G/GX
  calculator, various Mathcad versions, and Wolfram Mathematica 1.0 -
  i.e., treats Derive as one entry in a "vintage symbolic math software"
  preservation checklist.
- **marciot** (June 2025) announces uploading Derive 3.14 to the Internet
  Archive along with a full scan of the printed User Manual.
- **02k-guy** (June 2025) cross-links the new Internet Archive upload and
  a copy hosted on vetusabandonware.com.
- **druk13** (April 2026) reports trouble accessing the archived files and
  asks for the manual/disk images again; **SomeGuy** corrects a broken
  link, pointing to the working vetusware.com URL.

This thread is a good small case study of the retro-software preservation
community actively working, in real time as of 2025-2026, to keep old
Derive DOS/Windows releases and their manuals downloadable and runnable.

## PhysicsForums - two CAS-choice threads

See also `usenet-groups-google-threads.md` for full detail; summarized
here for the "forums" bucket:

1. https://www.physicsforums.com/threads/advice-on-choosing-a-cas-mathematica-maple-derive-matlab.38102/
   (Aug 2004) - a student asks how to choose between Mathematica, Maple,
   Derive, and MATLAB; the one substantive reply pushes Maple (for its
   openly published algorithms) and free numerical tools (Octave,
   Scilab), and is generally skeptical of CAS software's value versus
   "just learning numerical computation." No one in the visible thread
   volunteers firsthand Derive experience - by 2004 it had already faded
   from the live conversation among physics students, appearing only as
   a name in the thread title.

2. https://www.physicsforums.com/threads/open-source-mathematica-derive-like-program.150056/
   (Jan 2007) - a user explicitly asks for a free program "as intuitive
   as Derive," using Derive's UI as the implicit ease-of-use benchmark;
   thread resolves with the OP satisfied by Maxima. Good evidence that
   "as easy as Derive" remained a live shorthand for CAS usability years
   after the product's commercial death.

## AnandTech Forums - TI-92 nostalgia threads

The TI-92 (1995) shipped with a computer-algebra module co-developed by
Soft Warehouse/Derive, so these threads - while about the *calculator*,
not the PC software directly - capture real firsthand memories of the
Derive engine's UX as experienced by a huge number of students who never
touched the DOS/Windows program itself.

Source: https://forums.anandtech.com/threads/taking-a-grad-level-math-class-ti-92-calculator-woo-hoo.1712721/
(thread started Oct 5, 2005)

- **episodic** (thread starter): excited to discover the TI-92 was
  allowed for a graduate survey calculus course, surprised that
  calculators had been banned in his prior undergrad/grad courses, and
  wonders aloud whether having one earlier would have changed his
  academic trajectory.
- **Pathogen03**: reports his calc class only allows the plain TI-83+,
  specifically "due to concerns about built-in algebraic solving
  capabilities undermining learning" - i.e. direct evidence of the
  "banned because it does your homework for you" institutional backlash
  against Derive-powered calculators.
- **MrCodeDude**: prefers the TI-89 over the TI-92, calling the TI-92
  "too clunky" - an example of the recurring complaint that the TI-92's
  larger size/keyboard was a real usability tradeoff for the extra power.

Source: https://forums.anandtech.com/threads/ti-92-ftw.1676491/
(thread started Aug 21, 2005)

- **TitanDiddly** (OP): bought a used TI-92 for $35 on eBay, calling it
  "Best calculator evar."
- Various replies debate TI-92 vs TI-89 vs Voyage 200; one user says
  mechanical-engineering students told him it's not mandatory but
  "makes life so much easier" for coursework; others joke about its
  size/weight ("a brick").

Source (found but not deeply fetched): techpoweredmath.com "TI-92 Review"
https://www.techpoweredmath.com/review-ti-92/ - author recalls inheriting
a closet of decade-old TI-92 units in 2007 while coaching a math team,
upgrading their OS and training students on them, and describes the
"intimidation factor" of carrying the large TI-92 into a math competition
against opponents using plain scientific calculators. Also notes the
QWERTY keyboard got the TI-92 (and by extension its CAS capabilities)
banned from "virtually every standardized test," a recurring theme in the
device's history: symbolic algebra was powerful enough to be considered
a *cheating risk* in testing contexts.

> powerful, surprisingly light and practical to use!"

These read as classic abandonware-fan reviews: praise centered on the
combination of raw capability and small footprint, explicitly framed as
surprising "by today's standards."
